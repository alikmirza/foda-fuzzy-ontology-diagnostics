## Measurement note — RE1-TT runtime, 2026-08-23

`results/runtime_tt_per_case.csv` and its two summary files are the only
artifacts in `results/` that are **not deterministic**. Every other entry above
reproduces byte-identically for a given commit; wall-clock does not. Re-running
`scripts/measure_runtime_tt.py` reproduces the shape of the distribution, not
the exact values, and the numbers quoted in §4.8 are therefore pinned to the
machine below rather than to the commit alone.

Measured on: Apple M4, 10 cores, 16 GB RAM, macOS 26.3 (arm64), CPython 3.9.6.
Single process, no warm pool, machine otherwise idle.

Protocol: 125 RE1-TT cases (64 services), 5 repeats per case, per-case value =
median of repeats, aggregates = mean and 95th percentile over the 125 per-case
medians. The clock covers the five FCP phases — onset detection, fuzzification,
Mamdani inference, topology inference, propagation, ranking and explanation
assembly. Case loading and schema normalization are harness I/O and are timed
into a separate `normalize_ms` column rather than folded in or dropped. Onset
detection is inside the diagnostic boundary because under Deviation 0 it is the
method's own work, not an oracle the harness supplies.

Headline: mean 344.7 ms, p95 354.9 ms per case; normalization a further
22.9 ms mean.

## Measurement note — RE1-TT runtime by phase, 2026-10-19

`results/runtime_tt_phases_per_case.csv` and `results/runtime_tt_phases_summary.json`
break the per-case time into the seven phases of the diagnostic path, timed
separately: onset detection, fuzzification, Mamdani inference, topology
inference, propagation, ranking and explanation assembly. Same machine and
protocol as the end-to-end measurement above (125 cases, 5 repeats, per-case
median), but a separate run: the phase medians are taken independently, so
their sum (328.1 ms) is not expected to equal the end-to-end mean (344.7 ms).
Like the end-to-end files these are wall-clock and **not deterministic**; the
shares, not the absolute values, are what the paper quotes.

## Measurement note — inferred-graph statistics, 2026-10-19

Theorem 1 (global contraction, ρ ≤ δ) assumes every row sum Σ_t w(s,t) ≤ 1.
`scripts/regenerate_graph_statistics.py` measures the graphs FODA-FCP actually
infers on RCAEval at the shipped configuration and finds that assumption
almost never holds: every row sum is ≤ 1 in 7 / 2 / 0 of the 125 cases on
RE1-OB / RE1-SS / RE1-TT, and the median of the largest row sum per case is
3.48 / 5.56 / 23.85 (maximum 41.27 on RE1-TT). The graphs are cyclic in
68 / 106 / 122 cases, so the Jacobi path runs on most of them. It converges in
every case without reaching MAX_ITER (median 6.5 / 7 / 6 rounds, maximum
57 / 34 / 25), and no iterate decreases by more than 1.2e-16, i.e. the
iteration is monotone up to floating-point rounding. Every per-case confidence
vector is asserted equal to `FodaFCPMethod.diagnose_normalized` before it is
recorded.

## Analysis note — interval estimates, paired tests and the FODA-FCP case audit, 2026-10-19

`scripts/regenerate_ranking_statistics.py` reads committed per-case files only.
Its case-level percentile bootstrap reuses `scripts/bootstrap_ac1_ci.py` and
reproduces the published Table 1a–c intervals exactly. The cluster bootstrap
resamples the 25 (root-cause service, fault type) scenarios per system, since
the five repetitions of a scenario are not independent. The chance test is an
exact two-sided binomial test against 1/|services|; for FODA-FCP on RE1-TT
(1/125 against 1/64) it gives p = 1.0, so the data do not support a
"below chance" claim in either direction.

`scripts/regenerate_fcp_case_audit.py` re-runs FODA-FCP at δ = 0.85 and
asserts that its pessimistic AC@1 equals both the week2 file and the
delta-sweep file at δ = 0.85, and that its SG, SC and EC equal the sweep, case
by case, before writing anything. The fault-type mappings it scores against
(strict and lenient) are stated in the script as a reviewable judgement.

Environment for the two scripts: CPython 3.9.6, numpy 2.0.2, pandas 2.3.3,
scipy 1.13.1, owlready2 0.51, rapidfuzz 3.13.0. Two consecutive runs produced
byte-identical files.

## Analysis note — propagation variants and the saturation bound, 2026-11-02

`scripts/run_propagation_variants.py` re-implements the propagation step with a
pluggable t-conorm / t-norm pair, optional row-normalised weights and optional
ontology gating, and asserts that the shipped configuration reproduces
`delta_sweep_per_case_*.csv` exactly (AC@1, refined AC@1, SG, SC, EC) at every δ
before writing. Main findings: under random tie-breaking (expected AC@1) no
variant beats local-only ranking by more than about 0.03 (0.578 / 0.420 / 0.435
on RE1-OB / RE1-SS / RE1-TT); the pessimistic-AC@1 advantage of δ = 0.001 over
local-only (+0.056 / +0.136 / +0.120) is tie-breaking that is no better than
random; row-normalised weights remove the collapse at δ = 0.85
(0.008 → 0.440 on RE1-TT); max and sup-min reduce to local evidence; bounded
and Einstein sums saturate; ontology gating has no significant effect.
`scripts/saturation_bounds.py` checks the bracket
(1−H)∏(1−δw) ≤ 1−C ≤ (1−H)∏(1−δwH) on every case and δ; its necessary side
predicts the saturated-tie counts (e.g. 89 possible against 87 observed on
RE1-TT at δ = 0.85, none below δ = 0.65, none on RE1-OB). Environment:
`~/.venv/foda-eval` (CPython 3.9.6, owlready2 0.51, rapidfuzz 3.13.0).

## Analysis note — learned rule bases, 2026-11-15

`scripts/learn_rules_experiment.py` compares rule bases for local evidence on the
same five z-score inputs. Environment: `~/.venv/foda-eval` plus scikit-learn 1.6.1,
macOS arm64. Design changes made after a first look at results, recorded here
because they are forking paths: (1) features for learned rules are clipped to
[-10, 10] after the unclipped trees split on z-scores of 1e3 to 1e15 produced by
near-constant baselines (the same floating-point residue as the product's O10);
the unclipped variants are kept as `_rawz`; (2) a no-rules reference
(`anomaly_max`) and a leave-one-root-service-out protocol were added.

Findings to carry forward. Learned forests of two-condition rules are never
silent (expert rules: 25-37 % of cases) and beat the expert rules on RE1-SS and
RE1-TT under every protocol (leave-one-system-out AC@1 0.560 / 0.600 / 0.528
against 0.496 / 0.272 / 0.344; RE1-OB not significant). Fuzzy expert rules beat
their crisp cut on RE1-OB and RE1-TT. A symmetric ramp on learned thresholds
does not help and sometimes hurts. Caveats: only five services per system are
ever root causes and they are the ones with latency telemetry (92-94 % against
35-77 % of all services), which learned rules can exploit; and the unclipped
"largest |z|" ranking scores 0.624 / 0.648 / 0.472, agreeing case by case with
the re-implemented CausalRCA (99-100 %), MicroRCA (97-98 %) and MonitorRank
(93-96 %) — those three adapters behave as "most anomalous service" rankers on
this data, and that ranking rests on unbounded z-scores.

## Analysis note — direction of propagation and real call graphs, 2026-11-22

Requested in review of the KES draft. In the evaluated code a service absorbs
support from the services whose anomaly follows its own (inferred lead-lag
graph); the papers call these "callees", which on a call graph is the opposite
direction. `scripts/run_direction_variants.py` runs both directions on the
inferred graph and on two real call graphs (Online Boutique as measured by Istio
in the product demo; Sock Shop's published architecture — neither measured on the
RCAEval testbed; Train Ticket has none), with three weightings and both readings
of ontology guidance. Tests resample the 25 fault scenarios, not the 125 cases.
Findings: on the inferred graph neither direction beats local evidence at
delta = 0.85 on any system; on the call graphs the papers' direction (caller
absorbs from callees) is worse than local evidence, while the reverse (callee
absorbs from callers) with unit weights and light damping is better: selected on
one application and scored on the other (delta = 0.3 both ways), expected AC@1
0.653 against 0.578 on RE1-OB (+0.074, 95 % CI 0.024 to 0.133) and 0.445 against
0.421 on RE1-SS (+0.024, CI -0.043 to 0.093); the gain is on delay faults.
Maximum composition equals local evidence for every delta < 1. 27 of 619,125
runs stopped at the cap of 100 rounds, none in the evaluated configuration.

## Analysis note — KES revision, remaining review items, 2026-11-22

Added for the review of the KES draft: an averaging operator (weighted mean), a
representable uninorm (a logarithmic opinion pool), the dual t-norm pairings of
the bounded and Einstein sums, capped normalisation, expected AC@k and Avg@5,
breakdowns, a failure split, a direct test of tie ordering, and brackets for the
Einstein and bounded sums. At delta = 0.85 on the inferred graph no operator
under any weighting beats local evidence in expected AC@1 on any system
(largest difference +0.016, RE1-TT, capped probabilistic sum, 95 % CI -0.022 to
0.054). The mean never saturates; the uninorm is worse than local evidence.
Raw weights at delta = 0.001 and normalised weights at delta = 0.85 are both
within noise of local evidence, so either near-zero damping or a weight budget
removes the collapse. Failures of the evaluated configuration on RE1-TT: 31 no
rule fired, 30 ground truth inside the saturated block, 59 below a saturated
block, 4 overtaken by an unsaturated service; on RE1-OB and RE1-SS nothing
saturates and the loss is displacement by unsaturated services (44 and 59
cases). Within non-zero tie blocks of H, propagated scores on the inferred graph
put the ground truth first no more often than chance; on the Online Boutique
call graph in the reversed direction they do (0.62 against 0.37). The fault type
an explanation names matches the injected fault in 6-10 % of cases (9-17 % on
CPU, memory and delay faults) for every variant. 129 of 454,125 runs stopped at
the cap of 100 rounds, none in the evaluated configuration. As a ranker of which
services saturate the necessary bound has AUC 0.999 on RE1-TT at delta = 0.85
against 0.995 for the weight sum and 0.979 for the out-degree; as a yes/no rule
it has precision 0.975 and recall 1.
