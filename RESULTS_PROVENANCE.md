## Measurement note — RE1-TT runtime, 2026-08-23

`results/runtime_tt_per_case.csv` and its two summary files are the only
artifacts in `results/` that are **not deterministic**. Every other entry above
reproduces byte-identically for a given commit; wall-clock does not. Re-running
`scripts/measure_runtime_tt.py` reproduces the shape of the distribution, not
the exact values, and the numbers quoted in §4.8 are therefore pinned to the
machine below rather than to the commit alone.

Measured on: Apple M4, 10 cores, 16 GB RAM, macOS 26.3 (arm64), CPython 3.9.6.
Single process, no warm pool, machine otherwise idle.

Protocol: 125 RE1-TT cases (64 services), 5 repeats per case, per-case value =
median of repeats, aggregates = mean and 95th percentile over the 125 per-case
medians. The clock covers the five FCP phases — onset detection, fuzzification,
Mamdani inference, topology inference, propagation, ranking and explanation
assembly. Case loading and schema normalization are harness I/O and are timed
into a separate `normalize_ms` column rather than folded in or dropped. Onset
detection is inside the diagnostic boundary because under Deviation 0 it is the
method's own work, not an oracle the harness supplies.

Headline: mean 344.7 ms, p95 354.9 ms per case; normalization a further
22.9 ms mean.

## Measurement note — RE1-TT runtime by phase, 2026-10-19

`results/runtime_tt_phases_per_case.csv` and `results/runtime_tt_phases_summary.json`
break the per-case time into the seven phases of the diagnostic path, timed
separately: onset detection, fuzzification, Mamdani inference, topology
inference, propagation, ranking and explanation assembly. Same machine and
protocol as the end-to-end measurement above (125 cases, 5 repeats, per-case
median), but a separate run: the phase medians are taken independently, so
their sum (328.1 ms) is not expected to equal the end-to-end mean (344.7 ms).
Like the end-to-end files these are wall-clock and **not deterministic**; the
shares, not the absolute values, are what the paper quotes.

## Measurement note — inferred-graph statistics, 2026-10-19

Theorem 1 (global contraction, ρ ≤ δ) assumes every row sum Σ_t w(s,t) ≤ 1.
`scripts/regenerate_graph_statistics.py` measures the graphs FODA-FCP actually
infers on RCAEval at the shipped configuration and finds that assumption
almost never holds: every row sum is ≤ 1 in 7 / 2 / 0 of the 125 cases on
RE1-OB / RE1-SS / RE1-TT, and the median of the largest row sum per case is
3.48 / 5.56 / 23.85 (maximum 41.27 on RE1-TT). The graphs are cyclic in
68 / 106 / 122 cases, so the Jacobi path runs on most of them. It converges in
every case without reaching MAX_ITER (median 6.5 / 7 / 6 rounds, maximum
57 / 34 / 25), and no iterate decreases by more than 1.2e-16, i.e. the
iteration is monotone up to floating-point rounding. Every per-case confidence
vector is asserted equal to `FodaFCPMethod.diagnose_normalized` before it is
recorded.

## Analysis note — interval estimates, paired tests and the FODA-FCP case audit, 2026-10-19

`scripts/regenerate_ranking_statistics.py` reads committed per-case files only.
Its case-level percentile bootstrap reuses `scripts/bootstrap_ac1_ci.py` and
reproduces the published Table 1a–c intervals exactly. The cluster bootstrap
resamples the 25 (root-cause service, fault type) scenarios per system, since
the five repetitions of a scenario are not independent. The chance test is an
exact two-sided binomial test against 1/|services|; for FODA-FCP on RE1-TT
(1/125 against 1/64) it gives p = 1.0, so the data do not support a
"below chance" claim in either direction.

`scripts/regenerate_fcp_case_audit.py` re-runs FODA-FCP at δ = 0.85 and
asserts that its pessimistic AC@1 equals both the week2 file and the
delta-sweep file at δ = 0.85, and that its SG, SC and EC equal the sweep, case
by case, before writing anything. The fault-type mappings it scores against
(strict and lenient) are stated in the script as a reviewable judgement.

Environment for the two scripts: CPython 3.9.6, numpy 2.0.2, pandas 2.3.3,
scipy 1.13.1, owlready2 0.51, rapidfuzz 3.13.0. Two consecutive runs produced
byte-identical files.
